"No more abrupt than the passage from twilight to darkness" and "seeped in, molecule by molecule, like salt into an incoming tide" are poetic elaborations the original never indulges in. "Smuggled in," "footprints in behaviour," "calling-cards in the fibres" create a sustained metaphorical register foreign to the original. The phrase "not from sentimental fondness for earth-worms" has a wit and self-consciousness absent from the original. "The principle of continuity is ambidextrous" personifies in a way the original doesn't. The "Victorian parliamentary debate" joke at the end is too self-deprecating and humorous. While brilliant writing, this feels like a more literary, more performative author than the original—someone writing with more conscious style.

**Text D Analysis:**
This is the most dramatically different. "Peel back the mind's shimmering skin" opens with visceral, almost violent imagery. "Single-celled drifter," "embarrassingly narrow," "palimpsest of the original slime"—all far too colorful. "Immaterial jet-propulsion kit" is jarringly modern and colloquial. "Smoking-gun discontinuity" uses detective-fiction vocabulary. "The one specimen I can taste from the inside" is startlingly sensory. "Dim editions of myself," "Byzantine manuscript of human subjectivity," "Rosetta stones for decrypting"—these sustained metaphors create a voice that's trying hard to be vivid and memorable. "Seats still warm" is too casual and jokey. The entire piece reads like someone deliberately trying to make philosophy exciting and accessible through colorful language—the opposite of the original's restrained, methodical approach.

**Ranking Decision:**
1. **Text B** - Most similar: maintains the measured, philosophical tone, careful qualifications, balanced presentation, and scholarly distance while remaining personally present. A few slightly more vivid phrases but overall the voice is remarkably consistent with the original.

2. **Text C** - Second: captures the intellectual sophistication and careful argumentation, but adds literary flourishes, sustained metaphors, and self-conscious wit that make it feel like a more performative version of a similar authorial personality.

3. **Text A** - Third: has some structural similarities in argument but the tone is notably more sardonic and clever, with metaphors ("anatomical ledger," "republic of mind") that feel too theatrical for the original's restrained voice.

4. **Text D** - Least similar: the most dramatically different, with visceral imagery, colloquial modernisms, and sustained attempts at vivid metaphor that create an entirely different relationship with the reader—more populist, more performative, more concerned with being memorable than with being precise.
